# Voice

How the words on a slide, figure or screen sound. The pictures are dense and quiet; the words are few, plain and exact. One idea per slide, said once.

## Principles
1. **Ask, then answer.** A title is a real question the audience might ask. The answer line replies in one sentence. Everything else on the slide supports that sentence.
2. **Name the actor, then what it does.** Labels start with who or what ("The inspectors", "The component bay"), followed by one or two short lines in the present tense.
3. **Concrete over abstract.** "Paid per part delivered" rather than "variable compensation model". Put the technical term in the tag, where it is a label and not prose.
4. **Calm and exact.** No hype, no exclamation marks, no rhetorical flourishes. Dry wit is welcome in asides ("The ship is processing. So should you.").
5. **Trust the reader.** Do not explain the picture in words; say what the picture cannot.

## Patterns and limits
| Slot | Pattern | Limit | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Title | A question, sentence case, ends with "?" | 2 lines, about 8 words | Who keeps the lights on? |
| Original-language line | The source phrase, untranslated, italic | 1 line | Fiat lux |
| Answer line | One declarative sentence; a semicolon may balance two halves | 1 per slide, about 12 words | Big firms build in-house; small firms buy in. |
| Callout title | "The" + actor, sentence case, no full stop | 3 words | The workshop |
| Callout body | Present tense, what the actor does or faces | 2 to 3 lines of about 38 characters | Thin reserves: one bad month and the lights go out. |
| Tag | category, a middle dot, the term, all lower case | about 5 words | role · small buyer |
| Break slide | One word, a time, a two-line aside | 1 + 4 + 8 words | Break / Back in 15 minutes / The ship is processing. So should you. |
| Credit | "After" + source and year, then what is stylised | 1 line | After Smith (1776). Firms, storms and reserves stylised. |
| Chart label | The thing measured, with its unit | 3 words | Reserve cover, months |
| Alt text | One sentence: subject, setting, the single light | 1 sentence | A person and a robot face each other across a glowing pane lit by one orb. |

## Mechanics
- **Sentence case everywhere**: titles, labels, buttons, table headers. Capitals only for proper nouns.
- **British spelling**: colour, stylised, behaviour, centre.
- **Punctuation**: full stops on sentences, none on titles of labels or on buttons. A colon may set up an explanation; a semicolon may balance two halves. No dashes used as punctuation: use a comma, a colon or brackets. No exclamation marks. No ellipses except for something in progress.
- **Numbers**: numerals for anything measured (15 minutes, 3 tiers); words for one to nine when counting people or ideas in prose. Units spelled out in prose, abbreviated in charts. Thin space or none in thousands (12 000), never a comma inside a mono figure.
- **"and", not "&"**, except inside a cited authors' names.
- **Quotation marks**: double, and only for words someone actually said or wrote.
- **Contractions** are fine in asides, not in titles or answer lines.

## Interface copy (UI layer)
- **Buttons**: verb first, 1 to 3 words ("Open the bay", "Compare"). Never "OK", "Submit" or "Click here".
- **Errors**: what happened, then what to do, in one or two short sentences ("That is above the line's capacity. Enter 9 500 or fewer."). No "Error:" prefix, no blame, no "please".
- **Empty states**: name the space and offer the first action ("No suppliers yet. Add the first one.").
- **Placeholders**: a real example of valid input, not an instruction.
- **Status**: always a word beside the colour ("on plan", "at risk").

## Words to avoid
| Avoid | Why | Use |
|---|---|---|
| leverage, unlock, empower, seamless, robust | filler | say what it does |
| simply, just, easy, obviously | presumes | cut it |
| very, really, truly, incredibly | padding | a more exact word |
| successfully | the result already says so | "Saved" |
| utilise, in order to, due to the fact that | long for no reason | use, to, because |
| It is important to note that | throat-clearing | start with the point |

## Check before delivery
- One question, one answer line, and they match.
- Every label names an actor and fits its limit.
- No word from the avoid list; no exclamation mark; no dash used as punctuation.
- Spelling is consistent with the list above.
- The slide still makes sense with the picture covered, and the picture still makes sense with the words covered.
